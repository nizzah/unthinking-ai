# SPARK
## Product Requirements Document
**Unthinking Space | UX Design**
**Version 2.1 | March 2026**

A Meaning-Tech application that helps users surface insights from fragmented knowledge and take small courageous steps toward a more meaningful life.

---

## 1. Context

The Spark project addresses a deeply human challenge: helping individuals break free from inherited mental scripts and rediscover authentic meaning and purpose. We have been assigned by the client to design a solution that will help users take small steps towards what matters and help people reconnect with their own wisdom.

> *"I'm not looking for a perfect answer, just honest experiences. Feel free to take your time. There's no expectation to have things figured out. I'm much more interested in the messy, real parts. If something doesn't resonate, we can move on. I really appreciate you being open with me."*

---

## 2. Product Overview

### 2.1 Product Vision

Spark is a Meaning-Tech application that helps people surface insights from their own fragmented knowledge, see patterns in their thinking, and take small courageous steps toward a more meaningful life.

It is not a productivity tool, a meditation app, or a journaling diary. It sits at a new intersection: self-awareness, knowledge management, and creative courage.

### 2.2 Brand Mantra

*"I unthink, therefore I become."*

### 2.3 Strategic Positioning

| Category | Why Spark is different |
| :---- | :---- |
| Productivity apps | Spark measures meaning, not output or efficiency |
| Meditation apps | Spark connects reflection to action, not calm alone |
| Journaling apps | Spark surfaces what is already captured — it does not just store |
| Coaching | Spark is always available, affordable, and grounded in the user's own data |
| Note-taking systems | Spark requires no system-building or maintenance from the user |

## 3. The Problem Space

Research across nine user interviews identified two distinct but connected core problems. These were validated in the client ideation workshop and agreed as the design priority.

**Problem 1: Understanding the patterns**
Users need the right insight delivered during the right moment because they lack the time and skill to manually connect the dots between their fragmented thoughts and notes.

**Problem 2: Getting over the fear**
Users want to act more fearlessly on the patterns they see because they trust these insights, but even when the pattern or clarity emerges they do not feel ready, because fear of failure and waiting for a good feeling keeps them in the thinking phase and delays action.

**Research evidence — Problem 1:**
- Ideas are captured everywhere but never revisited: notes, voice memos, screenshots, messages to themselves
- Notes quickly become passive graveyards: they hold meaning in the moment but are never resurfaced
- Manual synthesis is exhausting — users want the connection made for them, not by them
- Memory is unreliable — users cannot reconstruct what helped them during previous transitions

> *"I've got three years of journal notes and I still can't tell you what helped me through my last career change. I have all this information but I still don't really know myself."*

**Research evidence — Problem 2:**
- Fear and self-doubt are the primary blockers, not lack of insight or information
- Users wait for permission or external validation before acting on what they already know
- Perfectionism keeps users in the thinking phase — they wait for the right moment that never comes
- When goals are broken into the smallest possible step, the emotional barrier to action drops significantly

> *"My notes become a cemetery — unorganised, unconnected, and impossible to act on."*

### Competitor Analysis

| Product | Overlap with Spark |
| :---- | :---- |
| Day One | Journaling and daily reflection — strong capture, weak insight generation |
| Rosebud | AI-assisted journaling — similar emotional tone, less focus on knowledge connections |
| Reflect | Note-taking with AI synthesis — strong on organisation, weak on meaning and courage |

| Sublime | The stars-and-connections metaphor in practice — study its friction points |
| Screvi | A social-media style feed of your highlights and notes from Kindle etc. |

## 4. User Persona

### Lauren — The Awakening Achiever

Lauren is a composite persona built from patterns identified across nine qualitative user interviews. Every detail in her profile has a direct source in the research.

- **Age:** 35
- **Occupation:** Experienced tech professional
- **Location:** London
- **Background:** British European
- **Core quote:** *"I've got three years of journals, notes, and data on myself, and I still can't tell you what actually helped me through my last career change."*

**Her Mindset**
- Can I trust that what I think is true about myself is actually true?
- Something bad will happen if I put this idea out there

**Her Goals**
- Quickly navigate to moments of notes and reflection when an insight occurs
- Move her goals and ideas from background noise to reality
- Act more fearlessly on the patterns she sees

**Her Needs**
- A unified view of her data to prevent it becoming a cemetery
- Human validation and permission to move forward
- Guidance on breaking things down into small, manageable steps
- Control and transparency over how tools use her data
- Tools that feel local, small, and controllable

**Her Behaviours and Habits**
- She relies on external validation at decision points — friend, mentor, coach — but executes independently once she has that signal
- She breaks big things into the smallest possible action — a tweet instead of a blog post — as a workaround for her own avoidance
- She captures ideas readily but rarely revisits them
- Her quiet windows are weekday evenings and weekend mornings — these are when she is most receptive to reflection

**Her Pains**
- Fragmented system — notes scattered across multiple tools with no unified view
- Manual synthesis is exhausting — she wants the connection made for her
- Tools that require manual personalisation before they are useful
- Unreliable memory — she cannot reconstruct what helped her in the past
- Overthinking and perfectionism that delays action despite clarity

## 5. User Scenario

**The following scenario documents Lauren's first experience with Spark. It is grounded in research findings and represents the minimum viable emotional journey the product must deliver on day one.**

It's Saturday morning. Lauren is sitting on her couch with a coffee, laptop open but untouched. This is usually when she does her weekly reflection. She is tired in the specific way that comes not from doing too much but from doing too much of the wrong things.

She knows the week has been full of energising conversations, new ideas, moments that felt important — but now that she's trying to reflect, everything feels scattered. Her thoughts are spread across places: a few pages in her journal, voice notes from walks, half-written ideas in her iPhone notes, and some structured thoughts in Obsidian.

Normally, she'd spend time manually pulling ideas together — circling words, rewriting notes, trying to make sense of her own words. But today, it feels like too much effort. She feels tired. Slightly flat. And underneath that, something heavier: *"If I can't even make sense of this… how am I supposed to turn it into something real?"*

Then, she remembers the conversation she had with her friend earlier in the week about a new tool called Spark that helped her to spot patterns in ideas, thoughts, and reflections — and turn them into real actionable small steps.

Lauren opens the link on her phone. The landing page doesn't overwhelm her. It doesn't promise to organise her life or make her more productive. It says something closer to: *your thinking is already in there — let's find it.* That framing is different enough from every other app she has tried that she keeps reading.

She signs up.

Then comes the moment that will either make or break Spark for her: the app asks her to connect her notes. And here is where she hesitates. She doesn't want another complex system to manage, or another tech company using her data for their own benefit. Spark seems like a small organisation that she can trust. She's happy to see that they are being transparent about what happens to her data, and she has the option to control privacy settings.

She takes some photos of her journals, and screenshots from her notes from the week, and uploads them to Spark. Just sharing this with something else has made her feel lighter.

Spark shows her a simple screen. It doesn't ask her to organise anything. It asks her one question: *"What is on your mind right now?"* She types, almost without thinking: *"I feel stuck. I've had loads of ideas this week but I can't connect them. I want to start a podcast but I don't know if my idea is good enough and I keep putting it off."* There's relief in getting it out of her head.

Then Spark shows her something she did not expect. It doesn't show her a dashboard or a graph. It visualises a pattern she hadn't noticed before — Resilience in uncertain moments, fear of being seen, moments of confidence in conversations.

Lauren stops. This is the first time her thoughts feel connected instead of lost. She has been circling this for months without naming it. It was there the whole time, buried in her own words. Spark doesn't tell her what to do. It asks: *does this feel true?* She taps yes.

Then something she hasn't seen in any other app appears. Spark shows her a ladder of 5 small steps. Not a to-do list, not a project plan — a simple visual path from where she is right now to the version of herself that has already done this. The steps are gentle enough that none of them feel like the scary thing she has been avoiding.

She taps the first one. Spark has suggested the simplest possible next step: *write one sentence about what you want people to feel when they listen. Take two minutes to do this.* She has the option to do a guided visualisation of what it could feel like to complete this step.

She does it. Right there, on her phone, on a Saturday morning. She closes the app feeling lighter than she has in weeks — not because she solved anything, but because something that felt like background noise now has a shape. She ticks off the step. Spark congratulates her, and reminds her to take some time for herself today.

On Monday, Spark nudges her. *"Ready for your next step?"* This time, she doesn't hesitate.

## 6. User Flow and Emotional Journey

If we had to pick one word to describe how the user should feel when they open the app, it should be **courageous**. Every interaction should support this.

| Step | Name | What the user should feel |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1** | **Discovery through trust** | A trusted friend shares the link. Implicit permission is what gets Lauren through the door; without it she waits indefinitely. |
| **2** | **Introduction to Spark** | Simple, calming, and communicates core value. Confident that data will be private. Curious and excited about what is to come. |
| **3** | **First input — one source, not all** | Connecting a single note source feels manageable. The fragmented system she is ashamed of becomes the starting point, not a problem to fix first. |
| **4** | **Second alternative input** | Spark asks "what's on your mind right now?" — bypasses setup anxiety. She has something to do immediately before the app has any of her data. |
| **5** | **Personal surprise on day one** | Something specific she actually wrote, returned in a new light. A moment of recognition, not a summary. Must feel like surprise, not onboarding. |
| **6** | **Confirmation and agency** | *Does this feel true?* Lauren decides. The insight only moves forward if she says yes. Spark reflects rather than prescribes. |
| **7** | **The ladder appears** | A visual path of small steps from her current self toward her goal, drawn from her own words. Navigable, not overwhelming. |
| **8** | **Quiet closure** | No streak, no score, no confetti. The felt sense of having moved is the reward. |

### Critical Failure Points

| Failure point | Risk | Design requirement |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Data connection** | Critical | If it feels like a setup or raises questions about data use, she closes the app. Language must be warm, precise, and jargon-free. |
| **First surfaced insight** | Critical | One generic result and she leaves. One wrong pattern is worse than no pattern. Specificity is not a copy problem — it is a design problem. |
| **Micro-action size** | High | If the suggested step resembles what she has been avoiding, she will not do it. Must be the tweet, not the blog post. |
| **Roadmap rigidity** | High | If the sub-steps inside a rung feel locked, the ladder belongs to the app. She must be able to add, remove, and reorder freely. |

## 7. Feature Requirements

The following features are derived directly from the research findings, the ideation workshop, and the wireframe review.

After downloading the app the user will land into an animated video + text intro to Spark, immediately communicating value. A quick branded animated transition leads into the first onboarding flow.

### Feature 1 — Onboarding
User will have the option to either Sign up, Log in, or continue as a guest.

### Feature 2 — Single Source Connection
Spark must ask Lauren to connect one note source, not all of them. The ask must be explicitly framed as a starting point, not a full import. Privacy language at this step must be warm, specific, and free of any legal or technical framing.

- Supported integrations: Apple Notes, Google Keep, Notion, Logseq, Obsidian, Google Drive, Dropbox, Claude, ChatGPT, Pinboard
- Explicit messaging: *"Your data is hosted locally — only you will have access to it."*

### Feature 3 — Second Input
Lauren then has the option to free write anything in her mind. Spark asks *"What is on your mind right now?"* This anchors the session in her present state and gives her something to do before the product has any of her data.

- Single text input, no character limit, no formatting options
- Voice input option for users in motion
- No required fields beyond this input

### Feature 4 — Day One Value: The First Spark
The first thing Spark returns must feel personally surprising. Not a category, not a summary — a specific moment from her own writing returned in a new light. This is the product's most critical moment.

- Surface one pattern, not a dashboard
- Show the source note or notes that generated the pattern
- Display a short, specific narrative in the user's own language — not generic insight language
- Follow with: *"Does this feel true?"* with options to confirm or regenerate

### Feature 5 — The Courage Ladder
When Lauren confirms an insight, Spark generates a courage ladder — a visual path of small steps from her current self toward the goal the pattern points to.

- Three to five steps generated from the confirmed pattern
- Each step has a short label visible on the ladder
- A figure or visual metaphor connecting current self to future self (the star)
- Option to visualise how it would feel to act out these steps before committing

### Feature 6 — Completing a Step
When Lauren chooses to complete a step, Spark acknowledges it simply. No streak counter, no score, no gamification.

- Complete button activates when step is completed
- Completion screen: a quiet congratulations moment with a visual that connects to her journey
- Reminder to celebrate and take some time for herself today
- Route back to the ladder showing the completed step

## 8. Information Architecture

### Site Map

Landing/home page (after onboarding is complete) — Central hub to visualise constellation and navigate to key actions from the bottom nav bar.

The user needs to be able to:
- See patterns/themes ("constellations")
- Search their constellation
- Complete core actions

Core actions:
- Access recent sparks and steps
- Start a new chat session quickly

## 9. Design System

### 9.1 Design Principles

These are non-negotiable constraints that should inform every design decision. They are not aesthetic guidelines — they are philosophical commitments.

| Principle | What This Means in Practice |
| :---- | :---- |
| Meaning over productivity | Never frame Spark as a task manager, efficiency tool, or output machine. The metric is felt meaning, not completed actions. |
| Gentle action over transformation | Small, chosen, reversible steps. Never pressure. Never guilt. The user always has an escape hatch. |
| Self-trust restoration | The insight should feel like the user's own wisdom — not the app's cleverness. Spark reflects; it does not prescribe. |
| Calm, non-addictive design | No dark patterns. No streak guilt. No infinite scroll. Generous whitespace. One thing at a time. |
| Privacy as a design value | Data ownership is non-negotiable. The user controls their data. The app earns access; it does not assume it. |
| Interruption must be earned | Notifications are a privilege, not a right. Every interruption must justify itself with genuine relevance. |

**The North Star:** After every interaction with Spark, the user should feel clearer, more courageous, and more like themselves.

### 9.3 Typography

| Role | Font | Weight | Notes |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Headlines | Manrope | Bold 700 | Large scale, generous letter-spacing, single line where possible |
| Body text | Spectral | Regular 400 | Warm serif for reflective, human copy — insights, prompts, narrative |
| Emphasis / insight copy | Spectral | SemiBold 600 | For the spark insight text and key emotional moments |
| Labels / metadata | Manrope | Medium 500 | Small caps for tags, step numbers, timestamps |

**Typography principles:**
- Large headlines. Generous whitespace. One idea per screen. Text should breathe — never dense.
- The Manrope + Spectral pairing is intentional: Manrope for structure and navigation, Spectral for the warm, human, reflective moments. Never swap them.
- Do not introduce any third typeface.

### 9.4 Visual Language and Illustration Style

**The metaphor:** Every thought, note, or reflection is a **star**. When stars connect, they form **constellations** — patterns of meaning that reveal something the user couldn't see before. Spark's job is to help users see their constellations.

**The world Spark lives in:** A deep night sky, warm and vast. The horizon is a gentle green curve (earth). A warm sun sits on the horizon. Star particles float in the background. The feeling is cosmic but intimate — like lying on your back in a field at night.

**Illustration style:** Flat editorial illustration inspired by the work of Andrea Ucini.
- Bold flat colour shapes, clean hard edges, no gradients, no atmospheric blur
- Tiny human figure placed in a vast, calm landscape — conveying wonder, not loneliness
- One clear conceptual idea per illustration — never decorative for its own sake
- Slight paper texture on colour fields — warm, not clinical
- Examples of the right approach: a person at a crossroads under a rainbow; a figure walking a tightrope across a vast sky; a man looking up at tall trees; Superman unravelling a tangled ball of string into a clean line

**What Spark does NOT look like:**
- No photorealistic AI landscapes (no glowing atmospheric horizons, no fog, no HDR-style imagery)
- No glassmorphism or heavy gradients
- No meditation app aesthetic (no soft blurred circles, no breathing rings floating in white space)
- No productivity UI (no Notion/Linear dark chrome)
- No gamification elements: no badges, flames, streaks, score counters, progress bars, or leaderboards
- No cluttered dashboards — one thing per screen, always

**Illustrations bleed to screen edges.** They are never placed inside cards or rounded rectangles. They are the world the UI lives inside.

### 9.5 Components and Interaction Patterns

**Buttons:**
- Primary CTA: Warm Coral `#FF591F`, pill-shaped (fully rounded), full screen width, generous vertical padding
- Secondary: Ghost / outline style — white border on dark backgrounds, no fill
- Tertiary / skip: Plain text link, no visual weight — always available, never prominent

**Cards:**
- Dark variant: `#0a2d45` background, `1px` border `#1c4966`, `rounded-xl`
- Light variant: Warm Sand background, subtle shadow

**Text inputs:**
- Clean minimal border
- Large text area for freeform writing
- Placeholder text is poetic, not instructional: e.g. *"Write freely. Don't think. Don't stop."*

**Navigation:**
- No persistent nav bar on flow screens
- Always one visible exit: a back arrow or X — no shame, no friction
- Progress is never shown as a bar or percentage

**Motion and transitions:**
- All transitions slow and gentle — 400–600ms ease-in-out
- No snappy, bouncy, or aggressive animations
- Animations should feel like breathing, not bouncing
- The breathing/loading animation uses a gently pulsing circle
- All motion must respect reduced motion preferences

### 9.6 Screen-by-Screen Design Notes

#### Screen 1 — Landing / Entry Screen

**Lauren's emotional state:** She has arrived via a trusted friend's recommendation. She is cautious but curious. She does not want to be sold to. She needs to feel that this is different.

**What this screen must do:**
- Communicate Spark's core value in one sentence — without jargon
- Feel calm and immediately different from every other app
- Not ask her for anything yet

**Content:**
- Pagination markers at top (story-style, horizontal dots — 3 total for the intro sequence)
- Spark logo (spark icon + wordmark)
- Headline: *"Turn your reflections and ideas into action with Spark"*
- Sub-line: a single warm sentence, e.g. *"Your thinking is already in there. Let's find it."*
- Full-bleed illustration: a person looking up at a single star in a vast night sky — flat editorial style (Ucini). The figure is small. The sky is large. The star glows gently in Warm Coral.
- Primary CTA: *"Begin your journey"* — Warm Coral pill button
- Secondary text link: *"I already have a journal"*
- Sound toggle button (bottom corner) — minimal, icon only

**Background:** Deep Space Blue `#052135`, full bleed. Green earth curvature at the bottom. Star field.

**What to avoid:** Do not use a white or light background. Do not make the illustration a card or thumbnail. Do not use blue for the button.

---

#### Screen 2 — Video Demo of Spark

**Lauren's emotional state:** She is still forming an impression. She wants to understand what Spark actually does without having to read.

**What this screen must do:**
- Communicate the three-step value proposition visually, not through text
- Build curiosity about what her constellation might look like
- Feel like a preview of something personal, not a product demo

**Content:**
- Same pagination as Screen 1 (dot 2 of 3 active)
- Spark logo at top
- Headline: *"In a few minutes, upload your notes, discover new patterns, and take small steps forward"* — key phrase *"small steps"* in Warm Coral, Spectral italic
- Animated demo (3 acts, looping or swipeable):
  - Act 1 — *Upload your notes:* icons of note sources (journal, phone notes, Notion) flowing in
  - Act 2 — *Discover new patterns:* stars appearing and connecting into a constellation — the key visual moment
  - Act 3 — *Take small steps:* a simple ladder or stepping stones appearing
- Sound toggle (same position as Screen 1)
- Implicit CTA leading to Screen 3 (swipe or auto-advance)

**Background:** Deep Space Blue `#052135`. The constellation animation is the hero visual — glowing nodes connected by fine white lines against the night sky.

**What to avoid:** Do not make the animation a video player with a play button chrome. Do not use a white background for the demo area. The three acts should feel like the night sky coming alive, not a product walkthrough slideshow.
